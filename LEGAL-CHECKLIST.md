# Legal checklist

**I'm not a lawyer and this isn't legal advice.** This is a practical list of what typically matters for a site like this one. Both of you are students, so it's worth having a parent read this too.

---

## Already done on the site

- **Disclaimer page** at `/disclaimer/` — says clearly that this is a student project, not medical advice, and that she isn't a licensed professional
- **Crisis resources** on the disclaimer page — 988, Crisis Text Line, and 911 for emergencies
- **A disclaimer box at the bottom of every article and interview**, linking to the full page
- **Privacy policy** at `/privacy/` — covers Netlify's server logs, Google Fonts, the contact email, and states that nothing else is collected
- **Both linked in the footer of every page**

---

## What she needs to handle

### 1. Interview permission — the big one

Before publishing any interview, get written permission. An email is enough. It should say she may publish their name, job title, quotes, and photo on the site.

Without it she's publishing someone's words and face without documented consent. For doctors especially, some have employer rules about speaking publicly.

Suggested email line:

> Can I confirm you're happy for me to publish your name, job title, a photo, and quotes from our conversation on The Skin and Mind Project website? You'll see the draft before anything goes live.

Always send the draft before publishing. It's normal practice and it prevents problems.

### 2. Photos of people

Any recognisable person in a photo needs permission. If they're under 18, a parent's permission.

This includes friends in a "teen voices" photo.

### 3. Stock photos

If she uses photos she didn't take, check the licence. Free-to-use sites like Unsplash and Pexels are fine for this. Do not pull images from Google Images or Pinterest — those are usually someone's copyrighted work.

### 4. Research citations

Summarising a study in her own words and linking to it is fine and is normal academic practice.

What's not fine: copying whole paragraphs from a paper, or re-hosting the PDF. Link to the original instead.

Figures and charts from papers are copyrighted. Don't copy them without permission.

### 5. Medical claims

The disclaimer helps, but the writing has to match it. Safe phrasing:

- "Researchers found that..."
- "One study of 300 teenagers suggested..."
- "This research doesn't prove..."

Phrasing to avoid:

- "You should..."
- "This will clear your skin"
- "X causes Y" when the study only showed a correlation

She's writing about a health topic for teenagers. Overstating a finding is the main real risk on a site like this.

### 6. Her name and school

Worth deciding deliberately rather than by default. A full name plus school plus photo plus contact email on a public site aimed at teenagers is a lot of identifying information about a minor.

First name only, or first name and last initial, is completely normal for a student project and doesn't make it look less serious.

### 7. If it's a school or club project

Check whether the school has rules about students publishing under a school-affiliated name, using the school's name or logo, or representing the school publicly. Some do.

### 8. Anything that collects data later

The privacy policy currently says the site collects nothing, which is true today.

If she later adds a contact form, a newsletter, a comments section, or analytics, the policy has to be updated. If she ever collects email addresses from visitors who might be under 13, that's COPPA territory in the US and worth real research first.

Same goes for affiliate links or sponsored posts — those need a visible disclosure under FTC rules.

## For you, Chloe

Worth putting in your client agreement for this and every future project:

- The client is responsible for the content they publish and for having the rights to it
- You're responsible for the site working, not for what goes on it
- Written approval before launch

That's a normal clause in web design contracts and it protects you when a client uploads something they shouldn't.
